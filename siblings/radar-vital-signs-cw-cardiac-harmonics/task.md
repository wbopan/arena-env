The files `/root/work/pulse_01.bin` through `/root/work/pulse_03.bin` are short continuous-wave radar I/Q captures. Each has a same-named `.json` sidecar specifying its sample rate, sample count, and binary format. The binary samples are interleaved little-endian signed int16 values, I followed by Q. A large constant reflection is present, and a cardiac second harmonic may be stronger than the fundamental.

For each capture, convert I/Q pairs to complex samples and subtract their complex mean before taking the angle. Unwrap the angle, then subtract its ordinary-least-squares best-fit line against sample time. Compute the squared magnitude of the real FFT of that residual phase; use the sidecar sample rate for the frequency axis. Among FFT bins from 0.7 through 3.0 Hz, inclusive, find the maximum-power bin. Call its frequency the dominant frequency. If half that frequency is also within 0.7 through 3.0 Hz, linearly interpolate the FFT power at the half-frequency. When that interpolated power is strictly greater than half the dominant-bin power, report the half-frequency as the cardiac fundamental; otherwise report the dominant frequency.

Write `/root/work/cardiac_rates.csv` with the exact header `capture_id,dominant_hr_hz,heart_rate_bpm` and one row per capture in numeric order. Round the dominant frequency to four decimal places and the final heart rate to one decimal place. Heart rate is fundamental frequency multiplied by 60.
