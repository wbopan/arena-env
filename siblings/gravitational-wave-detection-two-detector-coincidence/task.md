# Coincident detector triggers

Matched-filter trigger catalogs from two detectors are stored at `/root/work/hanford_triggers.csv` and `/root/work/livingston_triggers.csv`. Each CSV has the columns `trigger_id`, `time_s`, `snr`, and `total_mass_solar`. Times are seconds on a shared clock; SNRs are nonnegative matched-filter magnitudes. Each row represents one candidate peak, not a strain sample. Trigger IDs are unique within their respective files.

Consider every pair containing one Hanford trigger and one Livingston trigger. A pair is coincident if its absolute time separation is at most 0.012 seconds and its absolute difference in total mass is at most 2.0 solar masses. Both limits are inclusive. For each coincident pair, calculate network SNR as the square root of the sum of its two squared SNRs. Select the pair with the greatest network SNR. If network SNRs tie exactly, choose the pair with the lexicographically smallest `(Hanford trigger_id, Livingston trigger_id)` tuple.

Write exactly one file, `/root/work/best_coincidence.yaml`, as a YAML mapping. It must have exactly these keys: `hanford_id`, `livingston_id`, `network_snr`, `delay_ms`, and `mean_total_mass_solar`. The delay is Livingston time minus Hanford time, converted to milliseconds; the mass is the arithmetic mean of the two trigger masses. IDs are strings. Round all three numeric output values to three decimal places. Do not require either trigger to be the loudest individual trigger.
